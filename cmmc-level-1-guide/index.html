Access Control (AC)

FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(i–iv)
NIST SP 800-171: 3.1.1 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(i) CMMC Level 1

AC.L1-3.1.1 — Authorized Access Control

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Limit information system access to authorized users, processes acting on behalf of authorized users, or devices (including other information systems).
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Authorized users are identified.
  • [b] Processes acting on behalf of authorized users are identified.
  • [c] Authorized devices are identified.
  • [d] System access is limited to authorized users, processes, and devices.

Sample policy language

"Logical access to systems processing Federal Contract Information (FCI) is restricted exclusively to personnel with documented operational need, verified service accounts, and managed endpoints compliant with organizational trust criteria."

What should you do?

  • Consolidate authentication under an authoritative enterprise directory (e.g., Microsoft Entra ID or Google Workspace).
  • Prohibit unmanaged hardware from accessing cloud or local networks using device enrollment and compliance policies.
  • Enforce immediate deprovisioning workflows triggered by personnel separation.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Access control policy, active user directory exports, and authorized hardware asset inventories.

Interview

Discussions with IT administrators demonstrating account provisioning and revocation workflows.

Test

Live verification that disabled accounts and unmanaged hardware cannot initiate active sessions.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.1.2 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(ii) CMMC Level 1

AC.L1-3.1.2 — Transaction & Function Control

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Limit information system access to the types of transactions and functions that authorized users are permitted to execute.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Authorized users are limited to the transactions and system functions they are permitted to perform.

Sample policy language

"Privileges within organizational software and operating systems must adhere to role-based access control (RBAC). Users are restricted from executing administrative or functional actions outside their defined job responsibilities."

What should you do?

  • Establish standardized role profiles (e.g., standard employee, project manager, billing admin).
  • Strip local workstation administrative privileges from standard user profiles to block unauthorized configurations.
  • Audit functional application permissions during quarterly access reviews.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

RBAC permission matrices, directory security group rosters, and local admin group audit reports.

Interview

Interviews with system custodians on how transaction rights are scoped and requested.

Test

Demonstrating that a non-privileged user cannot run privileged commands or access restricted repositories.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.1.20 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(iii) CMMC Level 1

AC.L1-3.1.20 — External System Connections

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Verify and control/limit connections to and use of external information systems.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Connections to external information systems are identified.
  • [b] Connections to external information systems are verified.
  • [c] Connections to external information systems are controlled or limited.

Sample policy language

"External interconnections—including cloud services, vendor APIs, and remote connection portals—must be inventoried, authorized by security leadership, and routed through inspected access control points."

What should you do?

  • Maintain a documented register of all external SaaS, IaaS, and partner connections.
  • Block unauthorized remote desktop and file-sharing ports at perimeter firewalls.
  • Enforce conditional access policies that restrict connections originating from unauthorized regions or untrusted tenant spaces.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Network boundary topology diagrams, external connection registers, and firewall configuration rules.

Interview

Interviews with network architects on authorization procedures for new external links.

Test

Inspection of firewall drops and gateway blocks against unauthorized external IP ranges.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.1.22 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(iv) CMMC Level 1

AC.L1-3.1.22 — Control of Public Information

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Control information posted or processed on publicly accessible information systems.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Individuals authorized to post or process information on publicly accessible systems are identified.
  • [b] Procedures are identified to ensure FCI is not posted on publicly accessible systems.
  • [c] Information is reviewed prior to posting on publicly accessible systems.
  • [d] Publicly accessible information is reviewed to ensure FCI is not included.
  • [e] Mechanisms are maintained to remove improperly posted FCI.

Sample policy language

"Content designated for public websites, marketing collateral, or public forums must undergo documented administrative review prior to release to prevent accidental dissemination of contract-related information."

What should you do?

  • Designate an explicit review team (e.g., Communications and Legal/Security) with sign-off authority.
  • Enforce write-permission restrictions on public CMS platforms, limiting publishing rights to authorized personnel.
  • Maintain a documented emergency takedown procedure for removing inadvertently released sensitive assets.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Public release SOP, authorized publisher rosters, and signed pre-publication review records.

Interview

Interviews with public-facing staff on their workflow for identifying and excluding FCI.

Test

Simulating a content takedown workflow to confirm immediate retraction capability.

Identification & Authentication (IA)

FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(v–vi)
NIST SP 800-171: 3.5.1 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(v) CMMC Level 1

IA.L1-3.5.1 — User & Device Identification

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Identify information system users, processes acting on behalf of users, or devices.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] System users are identified.
  • [b] Processes acting on behalf of users are identified.
  • [c] Devices accessing the system are identified.

Sample policy language

"Every user, administrative process, and hardware component must be provisioned with a unique, traceable identifier prior to gaining network or system access. Shared or generic accounts are strictly prohibited."

What should you do?

  • Enforce standard naming syntax tied to an individual’s identity (e.g., `first.last@domain.com`).
  • Assign unique machine accounts and certificates to physical laptops and servers.
  • Configure background services to execute using dedicated, named service principals rather than default system accounts.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Active directory listings confirming no generic or shared accounts exist within the environment.

Interview

Discussions with directory managers on user identification workflows during onboarding.

Test

Inspection of directory logs to confirm that all events are traceable to specific named identities.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.5.2 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(vi) CMMC Level 1

IA.L1-3.5.2 — Pre-Access Authentication

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Authenticate (or verify) the identities of those users, processes, or devices, as a prerequisite to allowing access to organizational information systems.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] User identities are authenticated prior to allowing access to organizational systems.
  • [b] Process identities are authenticated prior to allowing access to organizational systems.
  • [c] Device identities are authenticated prior to allowing access to organizational systems.

Sample policy language

"No session shall be established until the claimed identity has successfully passed verification through approved authentication mechanisms (e.g., passwords, certificates, or multi-factor credentials)."

What should you do?

  • Configure systems to challenge every user for credentials at logon before launching the desktop or shell.
  • Implement modern identity provider authentication across all web, email, and cloud endpoints.
  • Authenticate devices via 802.1X certificates or MDM trust validations before permitting network routing.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Password policy configurations, authentication baseline standards, and identity provider settings.

Interview

Interviews with system administrators on authentication enforcement mechanisms.

Test

Assessor observation of a logon attempt to confirm that access is denied prior to successful credential challenge.

Media Protection (MP)

FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(vii)
NIST SP 800-171: 3.8.3 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(vii) CMMC Level 1

MP.L1-3.8.3 — Media Sanitization & Disposal

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Sanitize or destroy information system media containing Federal Contract Information before disposal or release for reuse.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Information system media containing FCI is sanitized prior to disposal.
  • [b] Information system media containing FCI is destroyed prior to disposal.
  • [c] Information system media containing FCI is sanitized prior to release for reuse.
  • [d] Information system media containing FCI is destroyed prior to release for reuse.

Sample policy language

"All digital storage media (hard drives, solid-state disks, USB media) and physical documents containing FCI must be cryptographically wiped, degaussed, shredded, or pulverized in compliance with NIST SP 800-88 before reuse or disposal."

What should you do?

  • Utilize certified cryptographic erase or multi-pass overwrite software on retired hard drives and endpoints.
  • Deploy cross-cut shred bins for physical documentation within company facilities.
  • Obtain formal Certificates of Destruction from licensed disposal vendors for hardware leaving the facility.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Media sanitization SOPs, signed Certificates of Destruction, and disposal logs.

Interview

Interviews with IT operations staff on hardware sanitization protocols prior to asset decommissioning.

Test

Inspection of physical shred containers and verification of drive sanitization software logs.

Physical Protection (PE)

FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(viii–ix)
NIST SP 800-171: 3.10.1 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(viii) CMMC Level 1

PE.L1-3.10.1 — Physical Facility Access

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Limit physical access to organizational information systems, equipment, and the respective operating environments to authorized individuals.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Authorized individuals are identified.
  • [b] Physical access to systems is limited to authorized individuals.
  • [c] Physical access to equipment is limited to authorized individuals.
  • [d] Physical access to operating environments is limited to authorized individuals.

Sample policy language

"Physical entry to facilities, server closets, and workstations hosting organizational systems is restricted strictly to authorized employees holding verified physical credentials."

What should you do?

  • Secure all facility entry perimeters with physical door locks or electronic keycard readers.
  • Lock server cabinets, network switches, and patch panels in dedicated rooms accessible only to IT staff.
  • Maintain a formal roster of personnel approved for unescorted physical building entry.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Physical security policies, building access rosters, and photos of locked infrastructure rooms.

Interview

Interviews with facility managers regarding access revocation when an employee leaves.

Test

On-site walk-through testing door locks, badge readers, and server room access points.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.10.3 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(ix) CMMC Level 1

PE.L1-3.10.3 — Visitor Escort & Monitoring

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Escort visitors and monitor visitor activity.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Visitors are escorted.
  • [b] Visitor activity is monitored.

Sample policy language

"Unaccompanied visitor access to operating environments housing contract information systems is strictly forbidden. Visitors must be escorted and monitored by an authorized employee at all times."

What should you do?

  • Establish a single designated entrance for all arriving visitors.
  • Assign an authorized escort who remains physically present with the visitor throughout the visit.
  • Instruct employees to challenge unescorted strangers observed inside secure work areas.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Visitor management procedures, escort guidelines, and receptionist training manuals.

Interview

Interviews with designated escorts on their responsibilities when guiding guests.

Test

Observing the check-in and escort protocol in real time during an assessor visit.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.10.4 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(ix) CMMC Level 1

PE.L1-3.10.4 — Physical Access Logging

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Maintain audit logs of physical access.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Audit logs of physical access are maintained.

Sample policy language

"Physical entry to facilities processing FCI must produce an immutable audit log capturing visitor name, date, entry/exit timestamps, purpose of visit, and escort identity."

What should you do?

  • Deploy a physical visitor log book or electronic visitor sign-in iPad terminal at reception.
  • Archive electronic badge swipe logs for facility entries on a scheduled monthly basis.
  • Retain physical access records for a minimum of one year to support retrospective forensic analysis.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Completed visitor sign-in registers, badge reader electronic logs, and log retention standards.

Interview

Interviews with reception staff regarding log completion and record archiving.

Test

Reviewing historical log entries to confirm completeness of required fields (time, date, escort).

NIST SP 800-171: 3.10.5 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(ix) CMMC Level 1

PE.L1-3.10.5 — Physical Access Device Control

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Control and manage physical access devices.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Physical access devices are identified.
  • [b] Physical access devices are controlled.
  • [c] Physical access devices are managed.

Sample policy language

"Physical access devices—including brass keys, RFID badges, and biometric tokens—must be inventoried, tracked upon issuance, and retrieved immediately upon employee reassignment or separation."

What should you do?

  • Maintain a master Key and Badge Register listing card numbers, assigned holders, and access tiers.
  • Promptly deactivate lost badges in the access control server.
  • Change mechanical combinations or rekey cylinders periodically and following high-level personnel departures.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Master key/badge inventory registers, key return sign-off sheets, and lost token procedures.

Interview

Interviews with physical security coordinators on badge provisioning and offboarding retrieval.

Test

Demonstration that a deactivated badge cannot open secured facility doors.

System & Communications Protection (SC)

FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(x–xi)
NIST SP 800-171: 3.13.1 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(x) CMMC Level 1

SC.L1-3.13.1 — Perimeter Boundary Protection

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Monitor, control, and protect organizational communications at the external boundaries and key internal boundaries of the systems.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] External boundaries are identified.
  • [b] Key internal boundaries are identified.
  • [c] External boundary communications are monitored.
  • [d] Key internal boundary communications are monitored.
  • [e] Unauthorized external boundary connections are denied.
  • [f] Unauthorized internal boundary connections are denied.
  • [g] External boundary communications are protected.
  • [h] Key internal boundary communications are protected.

Sample policy language

"The organization deploys managed firewall gateways at all external network ingress/egress points. Network traffic must default to 'deny-all' and permit only explicitly authorized ports, protocols, and services."

What should you do?

  • Deploy stateful firewalls at corporate network edges and cloud virtual network perimeters.
  • Block all inbound traffic from the internet unless explicitly mapped to an inspected service.
  • Segment corporate IT resources from untrusted visitor Wi-Fi networks using dedicated VLANs.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Network architecture diagrams showing security perimeters and exported firewall access control lists (ACLs).

Interview

Interviews with network security engineers on rule change approval procedures.

Test

Port scanning perimeter IPs to confirm that unauthorized inbound ports are closed and non-responsive.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.13.5 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xi) CMMC Level 1

SC.L1-3.13.5 — Public Subnetwork Separation

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Implement subnetworks for publicly accessible system components that are physically or logically separated from internal networks.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Publicly accessible system components are identified.
  • [b] Subnetworks for publicly accessible components are logically or physically separated from internal networks.

Sample policy language

"Public-facing services (e.g., corporate web servers, public file drops) must reside in isolated demilitarized zones (DMZs) or external hosting environments, preventing direct lateral access into internal corporate systems."

What should you do?

  • Place any on-premises public web or mail proxies in an isolated DMZ subnet.
  • Enforce firewall rules preventing connections initiated from the DMZ toward internal subnets.
  • Recommend hosting public web services with isolated commercial SaaS/PaaS providers outside the internal network boundary.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Network segmentation diagrams, VLAN configurations, and cloud security group separation rules.

Interview

Interviews with infrastructure engineers regarding lateral movement protections from public systems.

Test

Pinging or attempting connection from a public subnet machine toward an internal IP to confirm routing is blocked.

System & Information Integrity (SI)

FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xii–xv)
NIST SP 800-171: 3.14.1 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xii) CMMC Level 1

SI.L1-3.14.1 — Flaw Remediation (Patching)

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Identify, report, and correct information and information-system flaws in a timely manner.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Timeframe to identify system flaws is specified.
  • [b] System flaws are identified within the specified timeframe.
  • [c] Timeframe to report system flaws is specified.
  • [d] System flaws are reported within the specified timeframe.
  • [e] Timeframe to correct system flaws is specified.
  • [f] System flaws are corrected within the specified timeframe.

Sample policy language

"Operating systems and software applications must undergo automated patch management. Critical and high-severity security vulnerabilities must be remediated within organizationally defined service-level agreements (e.g., within 30 days of release)."

What should you do?

  • Enable automated OS patching via Microsoft Intune, Windows Update for Business, or third-party RMM tools.
  • Subscribe to vendor vulnerability advisories (CISA alerts, Apple/Microsoft security bulletins).
  • Run vulnerability scanners periodically to identify unpatched third-party utilities.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Patch management policy (with explicit timeframes), update deployment reports, and patch logs.

Interview

Interviews with IT operations personnel on how zero-day or out-of-band security patches are evaluated.

Test

Checking software version numbers on sample endpoints against current vendor security baselines.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.14.2 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xiii) CMMC Level 1

SI.L1-3.14.2 — Malicious Code Protection

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Provide protection from malicious code at appropriate locations within organizational information systems.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Protection from malicious code is provided at appropriate locations within organizational systems.

Sample policy language

"Centrally managed anti-malware / Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) software must be installed and active on all workstations, mobile devices, and servers processing organizational data."

What should you do?

  • Deploy reputable endpoint protection agents (e.g., Microsoft Defender for Endpoint, CrowdStrike, SentinelOne).
  • Lock agent configurations to prevent local employees from stopping or disabling real-time shields.
  • Ensure email gateways scan incoming email attachments for viruses and malicious scripts.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Antivirus central console deployment summaries and tamper-protection configuration policies.

Interview

Interviews with security personnel regarding automated threat quarantine workflows.

Test

Attempting to disable local security agent protections on a client workstation to verify prevention.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.14.4 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xiv) CMMC Level 1

SI.L1-3.14.4 — Update Protection Signatures

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Update malicious code protection mechanisms when new releases are available.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Malicious code protection mechanisms are updated when new releases are available.

Sample policy language

"Anti-malware definition updates and engine releases must be applied automatically as soon as published by the vendor to defend against emerging threat signatures."

What should you do?

  • Configure protection agents to check for cloud signature updates at least daily or continuously.
  • Establish automated alerts in the central management dashboard for endpoints disconnected or out of date for more than 48 hours.
  • Force manual definition syncs on devices returning from extended leave.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

Agent update schedule configurations and centralized dashboard definition compliance reports.

Interview

Interviews with IT operations on how endpoints with out-of-date signatures are identified and addressed.

Test

Inspecting local endpoint antivirus client status screens to verify current definition timestamps.

NIST SP 800-171: 3.14.5 FAR 52.204-21(b)(1)(xv) CMMC Level 1

SI.L1-3.14.5 — Real-Time & Periodic Scanning

What does this practice require?

Requirement: Perform periodic scans of the information system and real-time scans of files from external sources as files are downloaded, opened, or executed.
Assessment Objectives (NIST SP 800-171A):
  • [a] Periodic scans of the information system are performed.
  • [b] Real-time scans of files from external sources are performed as files are downloaded, opened, or executed.

Sample policy language

"Systems must maintain continuous real-time on-access scanning for all files accessed or downloaded from external media or the internet, augmented by scheduled periodic deep-disk scans."

What should you do?

  • Enable on-access file interception (real-time protection) in endpoint security profiles.
  • Schedule weekly automated background full-disk scans during off-peak hours.
  • Configure automatic quarantine action upon detection of malicious payloads.

What evidence should you prepare, and how may it be assessed?

Examine

EDR real-time scanning policy settings and historical scheduled scan completion logs.

Interview

Interviews with administrators regarding quarantine alert triage procedures.

Test

Testing the real-time file scanner with a benign EICAR test string to verify immediate interception.

CMMC Level 1: common questions

What is CMMC Level 1?

CMMC Level 1 focuses on the basic safeguarding of Federal Contract Information (FCI). It consists of 17 practices derived from the 15 safeguarding requirements in FAR 52.204-21.

How many domains and practices are included?

The 17 practices span six domains: Access Control, Identification and Authentication, Media Protection, Physical Protection, System and Communications Protection, and System and Information Integrity.

What evidence may support a Level 1 self-assessment?

The appropriate evidence depends on the practice and your environment. It may include policies, procedures, system configurations, access lists, asset inventories, logs, screenshots, records, interviews, and test results.

Important: This guide provides general implementation and readiness guidance, not legal advice or a guarantee of assessment results. Organizations should evaluate each practice against their own systems, contracts, FCI environment, and the current authoritative CMMC, FAR, and assessment requirements.
