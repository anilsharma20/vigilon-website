Free awareness for Indian families

Beyond the workplace, we run public sessions on the scams actually hitting Indian households right now, starting with the fraud worrying families most.

Trending now

“Digital arrest” isn’t real, but the losses are

Callers posing as police, CBI, ED or RBI officials tell victims, overwhelmingly senior citizens, that they are under investigation and must stay on a video call while “proving their innocence” by transferring money. India’s Supreme Court took note of the trend in October 2025 after a 73-year-old woman lost over ₹1 crore to a fraud built on a forged court order. There is no provision in Indian law for an arrest to happen over a phone or video call, and a genuine investigating officer never asks for money this way.

If a call starts this way

  • Hang up. A real investigation is never conducted over a call.
  • Never transfer money to “prove innocence” or unfreeze an account.
  • Call 1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline) immediately. Fast reporting can help freeze stolen funds.
  • Report at cybercrime.gov.in and tell a family member before doing anything else.

The numbers behind the training

Awareness training is the cheapest control with the highest leverage. Most incidents start at the human layer, not the network layer.

Phishing is still the top entry point

Email remains the most common way attackers get an initial foothold. Fixing it is a habit-level change, not just a technical one.

One reported email can stop an incident

Fast, confident reporting from any employee is often what turns a near-miss into a non-event.
