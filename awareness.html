TRENDING NOW

"Digital arrest" isn't real — but the losses are

Callers posing as police, CBI, ED, or RBI officials tell victims — overwhelmingly senior citizens — that they're under investigation and must stay on a video call while "proving their innocence" by transferring money. India's Supreme Court took note of the trend in October 2025 after a 73-year-old woman lost over ₹1 crore to a fraud built on a forged court order. There is no provision in Indian law for an arrest to happen over a phone or video call, and a genuine investigating officer never asks for money this way.

  • Hang up — a real investigation is never conducted over a call
  • Never transfer money to "prove innocence" or unfreeze an account
  • Call 1930 (National Cyber Crime Helpline) immediately — fast reporting can help freeze stolen funds
  • Report at cybercrime.gov.in and tell a family member before doing anything else
